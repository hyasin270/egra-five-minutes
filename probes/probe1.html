What it tells us

  1. Segmentation of a continuous coach recording is solved-enough by a frontier audio LLM. We do not need the coach to press "next subtask"; one recording per child (or per block) can be cut by the model using the administration script's own cue phrases. This alone removes most of the tablet-tapping that costs enumerators 3–4 minutes per child.
  2. Letter-level and word-level scoring in Urdu is in the right range on 8 kHz noisy audio (68 vs 70 correct), but it is a single clip and a generalist model, not a phoneme scorer. It cannot tell ر from ڑ or ت from ٹ reliably at 8 kHz — exactly the confusions a per-item HITL tick-box must let the coach settle. A 16 kHz WhatsApp voice note should help; a dedicated forced-alignment/GOP layer for Urdu is still needed for item-level confidence.
  3. The model is a usable protocol auditor. It caught an unflagged deviation. That is the "coaches assessing enumerators" layer, automated — worth keeping as a by-product even in the coach-administered design (it will audit the coach).
  4. Cost/latency shape: ~$0.15 and ~100 s for 17 minutes of audio in one call. A 5-minute coach battery at the same rate is ~$0.05 and ~30 s — well inside a WhatsApp turn. Splitting per block (Urdu / English / Math) lets scoring run while the coach moves to the next block.
  5. Noise is the enemy, not the model. Fan at 12:44, teacher announcing lunch at 15:01, soft child voice. The product has to control the recording environment (headset / close mic guidance, a one-tap "re-record this item") more than it has to pick a cleverer model.

Not yet measured (next probes)

  • Item-level agreement across ≥30 recordings vs the enumerator AND the coach reviewer (the two humans disagree too — measure that floor first).
  • Same clip through GPT-5-class audio and Gemini Flash (cost tier) to see where the cheap tier breaks.
  • English ORF slice through Azure Pronunciation Assessment + SpeechAce (already integrated / keyed) for per-word verdicts vs Gemini's.
  • Urdu letter grid through an MMS/wav2vec2 forced aligner to get per-item confidence rather than a self-reported 0–1.
