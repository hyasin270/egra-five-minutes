Early-grade assessment batteries — a decision walkthrough

Purpose: give the operator a one-screen-per-instrument view of what each major early-grade assessment tests, how it is run, and what it reports, so we can adjudicate which items go into our 5-minute, coach-administered, AI-scored battery (English + Urdu + maths, Pakistani government primary, grades 1–5). Prepared: 2026-09-15. Companion to 01_EGRA_EGMA_structure.md (which holds the full EGRA/EGMA detail and is only summarised here). Claims not confirmed against a primary source are marked unverified.

1. EGRA (RTI / USAID) — summarised from 01_

Owner / reach: RTI International for USAID (2006–), 70+ countries; Pakistan versions under PRP (Urdu, Sindhi, Pashto), SRP and Sindh SELECT 2024 (7,248 G2 pupils on Tangerine). Target: grades 1–3. Purpose: system monitoring and impact evaluation; diagnostic only at aggregate level. Administration: 1:1 oral, trained enumerator, paper or Tangerine tablet; 15–20 min (Toolkit), 10–15 min for 5-subtask forms. Every timed grid is 60 s with an auto-stop if nothing is correct in the first line; 3-s hesitation = move on; self-corrections count. Score: rate per minute via score / (60 − time_remaining) × 60; comprehension as correct/asked. Reported as cwpm distributions, % zero scores and % meeting benchmarks (Urdu G2 "meets" = 60–90 cwpm; Sindh 2024 Urdu-medium mean 19 cwpm, 45% zero). Urdu: yes (PRP/SRP/SELECT); open design questions are letter-name vs letter-sound, aerab on/off, RTL first-line stop logic. Reliability: enumerators must hit 90% agreement with a scripted gold standard; field IRR 88–98%.

Subtask Items Timed Stop rule Measures
Listening comprehension 3–5 Q on a ~30-word read-aloud no none oral language
Letter sound (or name) ID 100-letter grid 60 s 0 correct in first 10 alphabetic knowledge, clspm
Initial sound ID 10 words no 0 in first 5 phonemic awareness
Nonword reading 50 60 s 0 in first 5 decoding, cnwpm
Familiar words 50 60 s 0 in first 5 sight/word reading, cwpm
Oral reading fluency ~60-word passage 60 s 0 in first line cwpm
Reading comprehension 3–5 Q (only text reached) no not asked past point read comprehension
Dictation / vocabulary / maze optional — — weakly validated

Good at: continuous, comparable rates with known benchmarks; component skills. Bad at: too long for 5 min; rates are meaningless if the 60-s window is shortened without re-norming.

2. EGMA (RTI / USAID) — summarised from 01_

Owner / reach: RTI for USAID (2008–; Core EGMA 2014). Target: grades 1–3. Purpose: system monitoring / impact. Administration: 1:1 oral, ~20 min (plan 25); the child must say every answer (pointing "is insufficient evidence"); 5-s no-response prompt; untimed subtests stop after 4 successive errors. Pre-2014 versions had counting, one-to-one correspondence, shapes and patterns; they were cut for length. Score: timed subtests as rate/min, untimed as proportion correct; no criterion bands in the Toolkit (countries set their own, e.g. Tanzania A/S-L2 80%). Urdu/Pakistan: SRP adapted EGMA for Sindhi (grades 3 and 5); no published Urdu EGMA norms found (unverified).

Subtest Items Timed Stop rule Measures
Number identification 20 (0–999) 60 s none numeral reading
Number discrimination 10 pairs no 4 errors in a row magnitude
Missing number 10 no 4 errors patterns / number line
Addition L1 / Subtraction L1 20 + 20 (sums ≤19) 60 s each none fact fluency
Addition L2 / Subtraction L2 5 + 5 (two-digit) no 4 errors procedures; paper allowed
Word problems 6, read aloud no 4 errors application

Good at: fact fluency and procedure separately; oral so reading does not confound. Bad at: 20 minutes; no geometry/measurement/data.

3. ICAN — PAL Network (2019–)

Owner / reach: PAL Network (17 citizen-led members); ICAN 2019 in 13 countries, one rural district each, incl. Pakistan (ITA/ASER: Toba Tek Singh, 1,616 children age 5–16, 1,198 households); ICAN 2022-SSA repeated in Kenya, Mozambique, Nigeria. Open source, 11 languages incl. Urdu. Target: one tool for ages 5–16, items pitched at grade 2–3 (GPF-aligned). Purpose: system monitoring for SDG 4.1.1(a), household-based so out-of-school children are included; not a classroom diagnostic. Administration: oral, 1:1, in the home, by trained volunteers; paper and pencil allowed; average 15 min per child (20–30 min per household with the questionnaire). Structure: 26 items (2022 report; PAL's site now says 36 in the current version), half number knowledge, half geometry + measurement + data display, arranged in three sets: Set 1 (counting, comparing groups, shapes, position/direction, length/capacity, time/calendar, data chart) and Set 2 (number recognition, add/sub/mult/div "Task 1") go to every child; Set 3 (harder operations "Task 2" and subtraction/division word problems) only to children who got the corresponding Set 2 task right — a gated, two-tier design, not adaptive item selection. Number recognition uses the ASER rule: 4 of 5 correct. Scoring: total correct; policy-linking set the Minimum Proficiency Level at 17/26 for grade 2 and 21/26 for grade 3; reports show % of class 2–3 children who can do "at least 1 task each on spatial orientation, shape, measurement, number recognition and ≥3 number operations" (Pakistan 2019: 32.3%). Reliability: no psychometrics published in the two reports read (unverified); PAL cites ASER-style volunteer training and recheck protocols.

Domain (share) Tasks (Set)
Number knowledge (50%) count objects, compare groups (S1); recognise 1-digit and 2-digit numbers, 4/5 rule (S2); +, −, ×, ÷ without and with carry/borrow/remainder (S2 → S3); word problems (S3)
Geometry position/direction, shape recognition (S1)
Measurement length/capacity, clock time, calendar day/date (S1)
Data display read a bar chart, "how many more" (S1)

Good at: broad domain coverage (shapes, time, data) that EGMA lacks, in 15 min, comparable across countries. Bad at: no fluency/speed, few items per domain, one score with two cut-points.

5. ASER Pakistan (ITA) / ASER India (Pratham)

Owner / reach: ITA (Pakistan, 2008–), Pratham/ASER Centre (India, 2005–); 2023 Pakistan round 151 rural districts + 123 urban centres, 272,370 children. Target: ages 5–16, tool pitched at Class 1–2 curriculum. Purpose: system monitoring (district estimates) and TaRL grouping. Administration: household, 1:1 oral, volunteers with 3 days' training, untimed; reading + arithmetic ≈ 10 min (Vagh 2012, India). The tester ticks the highest level reached, testing from the middle of the ladder and moving up or down. Languages: Urdu, Sindhi, Pashto, English; separate arithmetic and general-knowledge sheets; two sample forms per tool.

Tool Ladder (bottom → top) Criterion
Urdu/Sindhi/Pashto reading beginner → letters → words → sentences (Class 1 text) → story (Class 2 text) 4 of 5 letters; 4 of 5 words; sentences read "as sentences, not strings of words"; story "fluently with ease"; bonus comprehension Q at story level
English beginner → capital letters → small letters → words → sentences 4 of 5 at each list level; 2 of 4 sentences
Arithmetic beginner → number recognition 1–9 → 10–99 (PSL variant adds 100–200) → subtraction (2 sums, both correct, borrowing) → division (1 sum, right method + answer) 4 of 5 numbers; start at subtraction
General knowledge picture/"largest" items, 60-s items new in 2019 PSL guide

Score: an ordinal level per child; reported as % at each level (e.g. % of grade-5 children who can read a Class-2 story). Reliability: Hindi validation r = .90–.94 with EGRA-style fluency battery; test–retest κ .76; no Urdu mapping published (unverified). Relation to EGRA: untimed, criterion-referenced, 5-item sampling; "story level" ≈ 44+ wpm on grade-2 text in Hindi.

Good at: 10 minutes, any volunteer, one ladder the whole country already understands. Bad at: no rate, ceiling at Class 2, 5 items per rung so a single child's level is noisy.

6. Uwezo and PAL-ELANA (the literacy analogue to ICAN)

Uwezo (Twaweza/PAL; Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda; ages 6–16; household; volunteers; grade-2 level) is the East-African ASER: literacy ladder letter/syllable (5 of 10) → words (5 of 10) → paragraph (4 sentences) → story (6–14 sentences) → 2 comprehension questions; numeracy: count objects 1–9 → recognise 10–99 → greater-than (Uganda) → 2-digit addition → subtraction → 1×1-digit multiplication → division (3 of 8 items each). Same virtues and limits as ASER; no Urdu.

PAL-ELANA (Early Language & Literacy and Numeracy Assessment, 2021–; 12 countries incl. Pakistan via ITA; 9 languages incl. Urdu): ages 4–10; tablet-based, oral 1:1 (no child reading/writing needed); a three-stage multi-stage adaptive design — performance on the first stage routes the child to easier or harder blocks, ending in low/medium/high competency bands mapped to the GPF and ACER learning progressions. Literacy tasks cover oral language → decoding → reading comprehension in five levels; numeracy covers numbers, geometry, data, measurement, patterns in five levels. Item counts, minutes per child and psychometrics are not published on the pages read (unverified); rollout was 180 communities per country. This is the closest existing thing to "adaptive, digital, Urdu, 4–10" and the tool ITA will compare us to.

7. DIBELS 8 / Acadience Reading (the "one minute per skill" template)

Owner / reach: University of Oregon (DIBELS 8, 2018–; Amplify distributes) and Acadience Learning (Acadience Reading K-6, formerly DIBELS Next); the US universal-screening standard, K–8, three times a year. Purpose: classroom screening and progress monitoring (not system monitoring). Administration: 1:1, teacher, five 60-second subtests plus a 3-minute group Maze; whole benchmark ≈ 5–8 min in K–1; 3-s hesitation rule; self-correct within 3 s counts; discontinue if 0 correct in the first line (LNF) or first 5 (PSF/NWF/WRF); "gating" rules skip harder subtests for a child who scores 0 on an easier one. Score: raw count per minute per subtest plus a weighted composite; each score falls in a band — blue (negligible risk), green (core), yellow (strategic), red (intensive) — derived so that ~80% of children above/below the 40th percentile on an external reading test land in the right band. Reliability/validity: ORF concurrent validity with Iowa Total Reading .75 (fall G1) to .91 (winter/spring G1); accuracy alternate-form reliability median .79 (Technical Manual). Urdu: none; Latin-script only.

Subtest Grades Time Measures G1 end-of-year "core" goal (DIBELS 8)
LNF letter naming K–1 60 s alphabet automaticity 59+
PSF phoneme segmentation K–1 60 s phonemic awareness 61+
NWF nonsense words (CLS / WRC) K–3 60 s decoding 87+ letter-sounds / 28+ whole words
WRF real words K–3 60 s word reading 50+
ORF passage (words correct + accuracy) 1–8 60 s fluency 76+ cwpm; 91%+ accuracy
Maze (every 7th word blanked) 2–8 3 min, group comprehension G2 end 18.0+ (adjusted)

Acadience end-of-year ORF goals: G1 47, G2 87, G3 100 cwpm; NWF-CLS G1 fall 27 / winter 43 / spring 58.

Why it matters for us: it proves a complete K–1 profile in ~5 minutes is a solved design problem — one skill, one minute, one risk band — and that gating on the easiest subtest is standard practice. Bad at: no oral language, no maths, English-only norms.

8. MELQO — MODEL (UNESCO / World Bank / Brookings / UNICEF, 2014–)

Target: ages 4–6 (48–83 months), school readiness. Purpose: system monitoring of pre-primary; adaptable per country. Administration: 1:1 direct assessment by trained enumerator, untimed, ~20–30 min, plus a caregiver/teacher report. Structure (cross-country robust core, 12 countries, 24,533 children): 16 tasks / 84 items — early literacy 27 items (letter ID 17, listening comprehension 4, initial sound 3, letter sound 2, name writing 1); early numeracy 29 (number ID 10, addition/subtraction 5, spatial position 4, set production 3, mental transformation 3, shape naming 3, number comparison 1); executive function 27 (Head-Toes-Knees-Shoulders 12, pencil tap 12, digit span 3); social-emotional 1. Score: domain scores, no fluency, no proficiency bands (countries set them). Urdu/Pakistan: not found (unverified); Bangladesh, Tanzania, Lao versions exist. Good at: what a pre-reader can do (EF, oral language, spatial). Bad at: ceiling by grade 1; 30 min.

9. Literacy Boost (Save the Children) and Room to Read

Literacy Boost (2009–; 30+ countries incl. Pakistan — Allai 2010 baseline, PEPAS/ICT 2014 with Numeracy Boost; Urdu forms exist): programme-evaluation tool for grades 1–3, 1:1 oral, enumerator; subtests are concepts about print, letter knowledge (letters known out of the alphabet), most-used/single-word reading, then passage fluency (wpm), accuracy (%), reading comprehension, with listening comprehension for non-readers; the headline indicator is "reading with comprehension" = ≥75% of passage comprehension questions correct. Item counts and timings not confirmed in sources read (unverified). Reports % readers / % reading with comprehension. Useful mainly as precedent that an NGO tool with a Pakistan/Urdu form treats accuracy and fluency as separate scores.

Room to Read (Literacy Program endlines, 20 countries; SurveyCTO tablets): a 4-task EGRA subset — letter sounding (60 s, clpm), nonword reading (60 s), oral passage (~60 words, up to 3 minutes, cwpm) and 5 comprehension questions. Bands: fluent reader ≥45 cwpm, emergent 20–45, non-reader below; comprehension "met" = 4 of 5; grade-1 letter-sound benchmark 45 lpm. Sepedi 2017: project G2 mean 31 cwpm, 2/5 comprehension. No Urdu. Precedent for a four-task EGRA that funders accept.

13. What this means for our 5 minutes

The invariants — components in nearly every battery 1. Letter knowledge (names or sounds) — EGRA, DIBELS, ASER, Uwezo, MELQO, Literacy Boost, Room to Read, NAT-FL, Amira. The universal floor item; the only disagreement is name vs sound. 2. Decoding via nonwords or single words — EGRA, DIBELS (NWF/WRF), ASER/Uwezo (word rung), Room to Read, Amira. Every serious tool separates "can decode" from "has memorised". 3. Connected-text oral reading with a rate — EGRA, DIBELS, Room to Read, Literacy Boost, and all three AI readers; ASER/Uwezo have the rung but not the rate. cwpm is the one number every funder and ministry already understands (Urdu G2 60–90; Sindh 2024 mean 19). 4. Comprehension questions on that text — universal; 3–5 literal + 1 inferential; ≥75–80% is the common "meets" bar. 5. Number recognition — EGMA, ICAN, ASER, Uwezo, MELQO; and basic operations (addition/subtraction; ASER's subtraction-with-borrowing rung; ICAN gates ×/÷ behind them). Word problems appear in EGMA and ICAN only.

Unique to one family (drop unless we have a reason): phoneme segmentation (DIBELS PSF); maze (DIBELS); RAN, spelling, executive function (Amira, MELQO); shapes/time/calendar/data display (ICAN/ELANA/MELQO); dictation (EGRA optional, "hard to score consistently"); listening comprehension (EGRA, MELQO, Literacy Boost — valuable but slow; Sindh 2024 kept 3 questions).

What the 5-minute budget forces: the timed EGRA/DIBELS grids are 60 s each plus instructions; five of them alone exceed 5 minutes. Every short instrument in the table solves this the same two ways — gating (ASER/ICAN/DIBELS: stop or skip on the easy rung) and subsetting (Room to Read 4 tasks, Sindh 2024 5 tasks, Tanzania 5). A defensible 5-minute core is therefore: letter-sound grid (gated) → nonword or word grid → 60-word passage + 3 questions → number recognition → one timed addition/subtraction strip, with ASER-style 4-of-5 stop rules, and everything else optional per grade.

What AI scoring buys that paper never could 1. Rate on every item, not just the passage: with audio timestamps, ASER's ordinal ladder gains a cwpm for free, and the 5-item rungs can grow to 10 without adding tester time. 2. Accuracy and an error taxonomy (omissions, substitutions, self-corrections) as separate scores — the AI-reader convention; paper enumerators only tick right/wrong. 3. Comprehension answers in any language, LLM-graded (Henkel et al. QWK .92) — lets us ask more inferential questions. 4. Automatic time-remaining and stop-rule enforcement — the two things Tangerine had to build UI for and where human IRR is lowest. 5. Oral maths as first-class: EGMA insists the child says the answer; audio makes that the record, and strategy (fingers, paper) can be photographed. 6. Adaptive routing across grades 1–5 (ELANA's three-stage idea) without a bigger paper kit.

What AI does not fix: letter- and nonword-level ASR for children in a low-resource script is the hard case (Xhosa 6.9% off-the-shelf; ~91% only after per-item fine-tuning with 200–300 labelled clips), and ASR over-flags errors on weak readers. The coach's tap remains the record of truth per subtask until the AI meets the 90%-agreement enumerator bar (01_ §6).

Open decisions for the operator (the ones this walkthrough cannot make): letter names vs sounds for Urdu; aerab on/off in nonwords and passages; whether to keep ASER's ordinal levels as the reported outcome (government-legible) with cwpm as the secondary metric, or the reverse; whether maths gets 90 s (number ID + one operations strip) or 2 min (add ICAN's shapes/time item); and whether the TIPS folder is the TIP diagnostic — if so, its KG/C1/C2/C3 ladder is the grouping the ICT and KP governments already use and we should map onto it.
