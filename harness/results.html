0. Where the human scores come from

  • Enumerator (primary): EGRA_EGMA client N=2233.xlsx, one row per child: per-item flags (1 = marked wrong) for letters/words/nonwords/passage, 60-s milestone counts, comprehension items, maths item flags, per-subtask timestamps.
  • QA reviewer (the floor): Data.xlsx → Reviews: 588 EGRA/EGMA re-listens by 7 reviewers marking each enumerator score correct / incorrect / unknown.
  • 187 of the 206 unique recordings join to an EGRA/EGMA row with both layers (the rest are ASER-instrument sessions).
  • Audio is 8 kHz AAC ~12 kbps (SurveyCTO audio audit), classroom noise, fans, interruptions. Every number below is on that audio; WhatsApp voice notes are 16 kHz Opus.

The tablet tap is not the child's first word (instructions run 5–15 s), so "within 15 s" is the honest comparability band. Where it fails: letter grids (see §3) and sessions where diarisation merges speakers. In the product the coach's own cue phrase + one voice note per block removes most of this.

Readings: (a) once the window is right, rank-order and count agreement on words and passages is as good as the literature's r ≈ 0.96, even on 8 kHz audio; (b) STT under-counts (negative bias), most on the Urdu passage (−10 of ~60 words) — Soniox drops or merges words the child did say; (c) letters are the failure case: letter names come back in three scripts and get merged, so the grid window is lost 62–84% of the time — coach-decided in v1, exactly as the literature (Xhosa 6.9%) predicted.

Free-text STT over-flags 4–6× more words than the enumerator; SpeechAce is better but still 3 of 4 flags are false. Per-word chips need forced alignment against the known stimulus, not free transcription (research/07 stack: Omnilingual/MMS-FA + CTC-GOP for Urdu; Azure PA scripted for English). v1 shows counts and the audio; v2 shows chips.

SpeechAce as a counter (threshold 40): count r = 0.74 (words) / 0.87 (passage), MAE 8–11, bias −8 to −10. Median quality score for words the enumerator marked correct: 40 (words) / 46 (passage); for words marked wrong: 30 / 34 — separation exists but is weak on this audio.

Exactly the split you would predict: items whose question is spoken are gradable from audio; items whose stimulus is on paper are not, because the transcript never sees the numeral or the shape. In the product the item bank is known, so number ID becomes "did the child say 47" — a closed-vocabulary match. Cost $0.11 per child. Bias is negative everywhere (the LLM marks "unknown" when the child mumbles; the enumerator, who could see the child, gave credit).

5. What this changes in the plan

  1. Counts, rate and accuracy per block are shippable from STT now (words, passages, spoken maths with a known item bank); letters and per-word chips are coach-decided until the forced-alignment layer clears the reviewer floor.
  2. Segmentation must be designed out, not modelled: one voice note per subtask-group with the coach's cue phrase, and the model's 60-s clock from the child's first word.
  3. 8 kHz classroom audio is the floor, not the ceiling: every number here should improve on 16 kHz WhatsApp audio; re-measure on the W3 pilot recordings.
  4. The human floor is the calibration target: ship a subtask to "auto-confirm" when the machine agrees with the enumerator as often as the reviewer does (words/ORF ≈ 55–60%, letters/maths ≈ 85–90%), and publish that table per region each month.
